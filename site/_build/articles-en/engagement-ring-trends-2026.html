Engagement ring trends 2026: what people actually ask for

Published 2 September 2026 · 6 min read · by the Oneiro house

Jewellery trends are usually decreed from a style office. These come from somewhere else: from what people ask us for at appointments, season after season. Here are the five movements we see rising — and, for each one, whether it is worth following.

1. The pear, set east-west

The clearest movement of the year. The pear cut is not new; what is new is laying it across the finger instead of pointing it towards the nail. The result is more graphic, more contemporary, and paradoxically more discreet to wear — nothing catches on pockets.

That is exactly what IRIS does, with a 2 carat pear in an open bezel setting, at €1,700. Will it last? Yes. It is a compositional decision, not a seasonal effect.

2. The bezel setting, finally taken seriously

For a long time the bezel was treated as the poor relation of claws. It is coming back through the front door, for three very concrete reasons: it protects the stone, it catches on nothing, and it gives a clean line that ages very well. Hands that work, sportspeople, parents of young children: everyone gains.

SOFIA illustrates it well — a 2 carat Asscher merged into its gold bezel, with a discreet pavé, from €1,900. Will it last? Without doubt: it answers a real problem of use.

3. The Toi & Moi, back through the main door

Two stones facing one another, two stories meeting: the Toi & Moi is a nineteenth century design that never entirely disappeared and is coming back strongly. It has the advantage of saying something, which a solitaire does not always do.

Our version, MIA, sets two one-carat pears against each other on a curved pavé setting, from €2,260. Will it last? It is a classic disguised as a trend. No risk at all.

4. Empty space as a design element

Settings that open up, stones that appear to float, solids and voids treated as deliberate. It is technically demanding — the stone has to hold properly — and visually very strong. DAPHNE is our most accomplished example: a 2 carat pear suspended in the central opening, €1,620.

Will it last? Yes, but ask about upkeep: an open setting needs its stone checked a little more regularly. We cover that in the care guide.

5. Coloured stones, without the folklore

Fancy diamonds — powder pink, champagne, light yellow, blue — are leaving the niche. Not to be spectacular: to avoid white, precisely, when the classic solitaire does not feel like you. The softer intensities (Fancy Light) are the most requested, and the easiest to live with.

Will it last? On the soft intensities, yes. On highly saturated colours it is a personality choice — magnificent when it is yours, dated when it is a magazine's. We source both, to a written brief.

What is on the way down

To be complete: the very wide halo is receding, so are overloaded double-band settings, and the race for carat for its own sake is losing ground to better cut stones. That is good news — cut is the one criterion never to compromise on.

The advice we actually give

A trend is useful for opening up the field of possibilities, never for deciding. The right question is not "what is being done in 2026?" but "what will they wear every day for thirty years?". Look at the jewellery they already own, the width of their bands, the colour of gold they already wear. That is a better brief than any moodboard — and if you have one, send it to us, photographs and videos included.
