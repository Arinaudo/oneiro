Finding the right ring size, without giving the game away

Published 22 July 2026 · 6 min read · by the Oneiro house

It is the detail that panics everyone, and the easiest one to settle. A ring that is too big turns and gets lost; too small, and it will not pass the second knuckle. Between the two there is roughly half a millimetre of margin. Here is how to get it right — and what can still be fixed if you don't.

How sizes are measured, in France and elsewhere

The French size is the inner circumference in millimetres, minus 40. A size 52 ring measures 52 mm around the inside. American sizes follow an unrelated scale (a French 52 ≈ a US 6), and British sizes use letters.

The most common women's sizes in France run from 48 to 56, peaking around 52-54. Men's, from 58 to 66. Those are orders of magnitude, not a method: never bet on the average.

The four methods that work

  • Borrowing a ring they already wear. By far the best. Careful: take one worn on the ring finger of the hand concerned, not the middle finger or the thumb (the difference can reach two sizes). We can measure it from a simple photograph laid on a ruler, or you can measure its inner diameter in millimetres.
  • The ring sizer. We post one to you, free of charge. Ideal if the surprise is not total, or if someone close can try it on under the pretext of a bit of fun.
  • An accomplice. A sister, a friend, a mother: the safest method after the borrowed ring, and the most used. An idle try-on in any shop is enough.
  • Thread or a strip of paper. Only as a last resort: the measurement always comes out slightly tight, and you have to allow for the second knuckle. Add half a size.

What throws everything off

Fingers are not the same size at all times of day. In the morning they are thinner; in the evening, after exercise or in hot weather, they swell — the difference can reach a full size. Measure at the end of the day, at normal temperature, and never straight after a run or a long flight.

Another trap: the width of the band. A 4 mm band feels tighter than a 1.8 mm one at the same size. On wide settings we usually add a quarter to half a size. It is our job to think of that, but you may as well know.

The margin for correction, model by model

Good news: most rings can be resized afterwards, by one or two sizes, at modest cost. But not all are equal:

  • Easily resized — plain bands, or bands paved on part of the circumference only. OREA with its fine setting, DORA: no difficulty at all.
  • Resizable with care — bands paved over most of the circumference, like CYRA. The setting has to be reworked locally.
  • Very difficult, if not impossible — bands set all the way round. Cutting a full eternity band amounts to remaking it. If that is your project, the size has to be right first time: we explain why in the wedding band guide.

Our method, in practice
