Gold at record levels: what it really changes for your piece

Published 18 September 2026 · 5 min read · by the Oneiro house

"With gold at this level, should I wait?" It has become an appointment question. The honest answer fits in one sentence: on a classic engagement ring, gold weighs far less on the bill than the stone — and waiting will probably cost you more than going ahead.

Do the sum yourself, it is simple

A solitaire setting in 18 carat gold weighs between 2 and 4 grams. A plain wedding band, 3 to 5 g. A heavily paved piece or a wide band might reach 6-8 g, rarely more. And 18 carat gold is not pure gold: it is 75 % fine gold, the rest being the alloy that gives the metal its colour and its strength.

Multiply: weight of the piece × 0.75 × today's price per gram of fine gold. That gives you the material value. On a €3,000 ring, that figure stays in the low double digits as a percentage — a long way from the centre stone, which accounts for 60 to 75 % of the total price.

In other words: a 20 % rise in the gold price does not raise your ring by 20 %. It moves a small fraction of it.

What actually moves, and what does not

Moves: pieces where metal dominates — wide bands, bangles, chains, solid hoops. On a diamond hoop or a plain wedding band, the metal share is significant and you feel it.

Barely moves: solitaires and rings with a substantial centre stone. On OREA (radiant diamond, fine setting, €1,620) or CYRA (pear solitaire with a pavé band, from €2,600), the gold price is an adjustment variable, not the subject.

Falls, on the other hand: the price of lab-grown diamonds, year after year. The two materials are moving in opposite directions, which almost nobody says. We wrote a separate article about it.

The three trade-offs that matter in 2026

  • Slim down rather than give up. Going from a 2.2 mm band to 1.8 mm changes the material value without taking anything away from the look — on the contrary, a fine setting often makes the stone look larger.
  • Put the difference into the stone. If your budget is tight, the lever is not the metal: it is the carat, the colour and the origin of the stone. Half a colour grade less is invisible; two tenths of a carat less, almost.
  • Don't play the market. We see projects pushed back "until it comes down", then overtaken by a date that does not negotiate. The cost of a proposal delayed by six months far exceeds a few tens of euros of metal.

And reclaimed gold?

Yes, it is a real option. An inherited ring nobody wears, a broken chain, a band from a previous marriage: gold can be melted down. We do it regularly, either by deducting the material value from the quote or by physically reusing the metal in the new piece when its purity and condition allow. The result is identical, with the symbolic weight on top. Mention it at the first appointment, that is the right moment.
