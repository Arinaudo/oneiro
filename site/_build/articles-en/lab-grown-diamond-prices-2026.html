Lab-grown diamonds: where prices really stand in 2026

Published 10 September 2026 · 6 min read · by the Oneiro house

The lab-grown diamond has done something rare in jewellery: it has fallen, for a long time, and by a lot. Enough to change the way a budget is built. Here is where the market stands in 2026, what it makes possible in practice, and the trap to avoid.

The gap has settled around 35-40 %

After several years of rapid decline, lab-grown prices have moved into a calmer corridor. Like for like on certified characteristics — same shape, same carat, same colour, same clarity, same cut quality — the gap with natural sits at around 35 to 40 %. That is the figure we see on our own quotes, and it has been stable for several seasons.

On our models it reads very clearly: AMI, our trilogy, goes from €3,300 natural to €2,100 lab-grown. KYRA and its 2 carat oval: €3,400 against €2,600. SELENA, marquise and pavé: €3,200 against €2,360.

What the gap makes possible, in practice

Our clients use it in two ways, and one dominates.

  • Moving up in carat for the same budget (the majority choice). The budget that financed a 0.70 ct natural stone finances a 1.10 to 1.20 ct lab-grown one. On the finger, the difference is dramatic — far more visible than any gain in clarity.
  • Lowering the bill for an equivalent stone. Rarer, but unanswerable when the budget is the hard constraint, or when the ring is only the first step before the band and the rest.

A third use is quietly growing: everyday pieces. A customisable pendant from €1,000 lab-grown, earrings you wear to the office without thinking about it: here, affordability is a real argument.

The trap: treating it as an investment

Let us be blunt. Lab-grown prices fell because production increased, and nothing says the movement is over. A lab-grown stone bought today will probably be worth less on resale tomorrow, in percentage as in value. Natural keeps its geological rarity and an established secondary market.

That is not an argument against lab-grown — it is an argument against buying jewellery the way you buy an asset. A piece worn every day for thirty years has no need of a quoted price. But if the heirloom dimension matters to you, you need to know beforehand, not afterwards. The full comparison is here, without bias.

What has not changed, and will not

A lab-grown diamond is still crystallised carbon: same hardness, same brilliance, same GIA or IGI certificate, marked "lab-grown". Nobody will see the difference, by eye or under a loupe. Be wary of two narratives: the one selling lab-grown as a "necessarily green" choice (it depends entirely on the producer's energy) and the one presenting it as a fake diamond (it is not).
