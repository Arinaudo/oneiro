Natural or lab-grown diamond: the honest comparison

Published June 2026 · 5 min read · by the Oneiro house

The question we are asked most often in appointments is no longer "what carat?" but "natural or lab-grown?". And it is an excellent question, because the honest answer — the one we give you here — depends on you, not on us. Both are real diamonds. Here is what actually sets them apart.

First, what is identical

A lab-grown diamond is not an imitation (unlike cubic zirconia or moissanite). It is crystallised carbon, exactly like a natural diamond: same hardness (10 on the Mohs scale), same brilliance, same fire, same lifespan — eternal. To the naked eye, under a loupe, and even for most gemmologists without specialised instruments, they are indistinguishable. Both are certified by the same independent laboratories (GIA, IGI), against the same quality criteria, the famous 4Cs.

What changes: the origin

The natural diamond formed one to three billion years ago in the earth's mantle, before being carried up by volcanic eruptions. Every stone is a unique geological object, extracted then traced — at Oneiro, exclusively from conflict-free areas, in line with the Kimberley Process.

The lab-grown diamond reproduces that process in a few weeks, through high-pressure or vapour-deposition techniques. The result is chemically and optically identical, marked "lab-grown" on its certificate.

The price: a 35 to 40 % gap, and it pays to be clear-eyed

Like for like, a lab-grown diamond costs around 35 to 40 % less. In concrete terms, on our AMI model (a trilogy in 18 carat gold): €3,300 with a natural diamond, €2,100 with a lab-grown one. That gap allows two strategies: reduce the total budget, or move up in carat for the same budget — and it is usually the second that our clients choose.

Let us be transparent about the trade-off: the price of lab-grown diamonds has fallen steadily in recent years, and their resale value is less certain than that of natural stones, whose rarity is geological and whose secondary market is established. If you are buying a piece to wear and to pass on, that matters little. If the heirloom dimension matters to you, natural keeps the advantage.

Ethics: less binary than it is made out to be

Lab-grown avoids mining — a real environmental argument, to be qualified according to the energy used by the producers. Traced, conflict-free natural diamonds support regulated supply chains and the communities that depend on them. Neither choice is "the wrong one": be wary of arguments that try to make you feel guilty in either direction.

Our decision grid, in three questions

  • Does the symbol matter above all else? A stone formed a billion years ago carries a weight that a laboratory does not replicate. → Natural.
  • Is the budget tight, or is visual impact the priority? Same brilliance, bigger carat, lower price. → Lab-grown.
  • Are you thinking about inheritance and long-term value? Rarity and an established secondary market. → Natural.
