Plain, pavé or full eternity band: how to choose

Published 5 August 2026 · 7 min read · by the Oneiro house

It is the piece worn most, and the one chosen fastest. Usually at the end of the planning, between two caterer meetings. Yet three families of wedding band exist, and they involve neither the same budget, nor the same upkeep, nor the same compatibility with the engagement ring. Here is what you need to decide properly.

1. The plain band: no stones

Yellow, white or rose gold, 18 carat. The whole decision rests on four parameters: the width (1.8 to 5 mm), the thickness, the profile (court, also called comfort fit, or flat) and the finish (polished, brushed, hammered). It sounds like little: in reality that is where all the comfort is decided.

For whom: hands that work, people who never want to think about it, tighter budgets, and anyone whose engagement ring is already busy. Budget: the gentlest of the three by a distance — the value is essentially the metal and the craftsmanship. Upkeep: almost none. A repolish every few years erases the micro-scratches.

2. The half-eternity pavé: brilliance every day

Diamonds set across the visible half of the band, the other half left plain. It is the best compromise on the market, and the most bought, for three reasons: you see the stones, the underside is comfortable against the neighbouring fingers, and the ring can still be resized — since it can be cut and rejoined in the plain section.

Grain setting (stones flush, clean lines) or claws (more light under the stone, more relief): a matter of taste, not of quality. In our collection the spirit shows on the pavé band of CYRA or on the double band of LYNA.

For whom: most couples. Budget: intermediate, and very adjustable depending on the number and size of the stones. Upkeep: a setting check every 18 to 24 months.

3. The full eternity band: set all the way round

Set across all 360 degrees of the band. It is the statement piece, often chosen for a wedding anniversary or a birth rather than for the day itself — and it is also the most demanding. It catches light from every angle, including when the hand is turned over: that is exactly the spirit of our diamond tennis bracelet, transposed to the finger.

Three things to know before saying yes:

  • It cannot be resized. Cutting a band set all the way round amounts to remaking it. The size has to be right first time — see our dedicated guide, it is the one case where we really insist.
  • The stones underneath wear down. Contact with neighbouring fingers and everyday objects eventually marks them. That is not a defect: it is the price of going all the way round.
  • The budget follows the number of stones. A full circle means two to three times more diamonds than a half. Lab-grown makes complete sense here: a 35 to 40 % gap multiplied by twenty stones changes the bill.

Think about the stack before, not after

This is the advice that comes up most at appointments. A straight band does not always sit flush against an engagement ring whose setting drops low or whose stone is set lengthways — a marquise or an east-west pear, for instance. Three solutions: a curved band that follows the setting, an open band that nests into it, or a band worn on the other hand.

And the couple's two bands?

Matching or deliberately different, it is open. What matters is designing them together: the same relative proportions, the same finish, the same gold colour — or a deliberate contrast. Inside engraving (a date, initials, a few words) is free on request, and it is often what people remember most.

Our recommendation, in one line

If you are hesitating and the engagement ring is already generous, take the plain band: it will never date, and you can add a full eternity band later to mark an occasion.
