The guide

The 4Cs, explained simply

Four criteria are enough to describe — and to pay the right price for — any diamond. Here they are, without the jargon.

Carat — the weight

1 carat = 0.20 g. The higher the carat, the larger the stone… and the faster the price climbs: a 1 ct is worth far more than two 0.50 ct stones. A tip: aiming just under the thresholds (0.90 instead of 1.00 ct) gives the same visual effect for a noticeably gentler price.

Cut — the craft

The way the stone is cut: proportions, symmetry, polish. This is the criterion that creates the sparkle. An "Excellent" cut maximises brilliance whatever the shape — round brilliant, oval, pear, emerald and the rest.

Colour

White diamonds are graded from D (perfectly colourless) to Z (a visible yellowish tint). From D to H, the difference is invisible to an untrained eye — a well cut G or H is often the best beauty-to-price ratio.

Clarity

Every stone carries small natural marks (inclusions). From IF (none visible under a loupe) to I (visible to the naked eye). The right balance: VS or VVS — imperceptible inclusions, sensible price.

The choice that changes the budget

Natural or lab-grown: same stone, two stories

To the naked eye — and even under a loupe — nothing tells them apart. Both are real diamonds, certified and eternal. One is a geological treasure formed millions of years ago; the other a contemporary feat, around 35 to 40 % more affordable.
