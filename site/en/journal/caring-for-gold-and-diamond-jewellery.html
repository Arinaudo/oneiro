Caring for gold and diamonds: the simple guide

Published 1 September 2026 · 5 min read · by the Oneiro house

A diamond that has stopped sparkling is almost never a damaged diamond. It is a dirty one. The greasy film that builds up under the stone — cream, soap, skin oil — blocks the light from below, exactly where it should come back out. Five minutes a month is enough to change everything. Here is the method, and the three things never to do.

Cleaning at home, in five minutes

  • A bowl of lukewarm water (not hot) with a drop of mild washing-up liquid. Nothing else.
  • Let it soak for 15 to 20 minutes. It is the soaking that does the work, not the scrubbing.
  • Brush gently with a soft-bristled toothbrush, concentrating under the stone and between the claws — that is where everything collects.
  • Rinse in clean water, with the plug in (this cannot be said too often), and dry with a soft, lint-free cloth.

Once a month for a ring worn every day. On a heavily set piece such as a tennis bracelet or a diamond hoop, the difference after soaking is dramatic.

The three things never to do

  • Toothpaste. The most widespread piece of household advice, and the most harmful: it is abrasive and scratches 18 carat gold, which is a soft metal. The diamond is fine; the setting is not.
  • Bleach and chlorinated products. Chlorine attacks the gold alloy and weakens the solder joints, sometimes invisibly until the day a claw gives way. Take your rings off before the pool and the housework.
  • The ultrasonic tank bought online. Excellent on a sound diamond in a solid setting, disastrous on a slightly fractured stone, an open setting or a coloured stone. A professional knows which piece can take it; a thirty-euro machine does not.

What to take off, and when

Sport and weight training (impacts deform fine bands), gardening and DIY, housework, pools and spas, creams and perfumes — the last leave a film that dulls a stone within days. A good habit: jewellery goes on last in the morning and comes off first in the evening.

Conversely, you can sleep in it, shower in it, live in it. A piece made to be worn copes very well with everyday life: that is exactly why we work in 18 carat gold rather than more fragile alloys.

Storage, which matters more than people think

Diamond is the hardest material there is: 10 on the Mohs scale. Which means a diamond scratches everything else, including other diamonds. Never keep your pieces loose in the same box. Each one in its own pouch or compartment, and pendants hung rather than laid flat so the chain does not tangle.

The setting check: the one appointment that is not optional

Once a year, or every 18 months for a piece worn rarely, have the claws and stones checked. It is quick, it costs little, and it is the only thing that prevents the scenario everyone dreads. The warning signs you can spot yourself: a stone that moves if you tap the ring near your ear, a claw that catches on clothing, a pavé that looks uneven.

Open settings and very fine pavé work need a little more attention: that is the price of their lightness. At Oneiro the check is included, and repolishing is quoted — the full guarantees are here.

An antique or inherited piece?

Do not clean it yourself before showing it to us. Antique settings, treated stones and low-purity metals all react differently, and some patinas are better left alone. Simply send us a few photographs, or a short video: we will tell you what to do, and whether the piece deserves to be reinterpreted rather than restored.
