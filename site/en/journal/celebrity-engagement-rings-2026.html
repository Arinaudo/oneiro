The celebrity engagement rings of 2026, decoded

Published 22 September 2026 · 9 min read · by the Oneiro house

A celebrity ring is not a model to copy. It is an indicator. When five widely discussed rings share the same shape or the same setting in the same year, it is not a coincidence: something has shifted in common taste, and it reaches our appointments six to twelve months later. Here is what 2026 produced, what each ring says technically, and what the same decisions cost when you are not paying for Place Vendôme.

A useful note: Oneiro has no connection to the people or houses mentioned here. We are commenting on jewellery made public by the press, and we reproduce nobody's design. What transposes are generic choices — a stone shape, a type of setting, a colour of gold.

1. The elongated cushion, the shape of the year

This is the clearest common thread of 2026. Zoë Kravitz, whose engagement to Harry Styles was reported in April, wears an elongated cushion in a bezel setting on yellow gold. Madison Beer, engaged this summer to Justin Herbert, wears an ultra-elongated cushion mounted as a solitaire on a slim band.

The elongated cushion is the compromise many people are looking for without being able to name it: the softened corners and warm fire of the classic cushion, plus the finger-lengthening effect usually sought in an oval. It looks larger than a round of the same weight, and it ages well because it belongs to no particular decade.

In the collection, the logic of a cushion brought forward appears on DELIA (cushion centre with a set halo, from €2,650). For the proportions to watch on this shape, see our shapes guide.

2. Bezel settings and yellow gold, everywhere

The second strong signal, and it matches what we have been seeing for a year: the bezel is out of purgatory. Zoë Kravitz's ring commits to it fully, in yellow gold. Zendaya's ring, by Jessica McCormack and revealed in early 2025, sits in a Georgian-inspired "button back" setting — the stone laid east-west on 18 carat yellow gold. Two closely watched rings, and the same refusal of high claws twice.

This is not styling for its own sake. A bezel protects the stone, catches on nothing, and gives a clean line that copes with daily life. Which is why it wins every time it comes back.

With us, SOFIA takes the logic to its conclusion — an Asscher merged into its gold bezel, from €1,900 — and IRIS combines an open bezel with a stone laid across the finger, at €1,700.

3. The XXL oval, with colour hidden underneath

The most spectacular ring of the year is probably Aryna Sabalenka's, engaged in March to Georgios Frangulis: a 12 carat oval by Isabela Grutman, with a halo of emeralds concealed beneath the stone. The trade press estimates it between $500,000 and $800,000.

Remember the detail rather than the carat: the hidden halo. It is an idea that transposes to any budget — a few small stones set under the centre stone, invisible from the front, visible only in profile and to the person wearing the ring. It is exactly the kind of personalisation we are glad to add on a bespoke creation, and it costs almost nothing compared with what it brings.

For a generous oval on a pavé setting, KYRA (2 carats, from €2,600) is our direct equivalent. And if the idea of colour appeals, our Diamonds section sources fancy stones to a written brief.

4. The marquise, a quiet but real return

Josh Groban and Natalie McQueen became engaged in May with a 4 carat marquise on 18 carat yellow gold, by Tiffany Chao. The marquise remains a divisive shape — you love it or you don't — but it offers the best visible-surface-per-carat ratio of any cut. For the same budget, it is the stone that looks largest.

SELENA, our 1.75 carat marquise on a pavé setting, starts at €2,360.

5. Vintage, and coloured stones

Halle Berry announced her engagement to Van Hunt in February with an antique ring from the 1940s-50s Retro period — a round centre diamond surrounded by step-cut blue sapphires, on yellow gold. Dove Cameron, engaged in January to Damiano David, wears a vintage-inspired Asscher.

The message is the same in both cases: white-on-white is no longer compulsory, and antique is no longer a fallback. If that direction speaks to you, two paths exist with us: a coloured stone sourced for you, or the reinterpretation of a family piece. Do not clean an antique piece before showing it to us — we explain why here.

6. The counter-current: the plain round solitaire

Amid the 10 and 12 carats, the most discussed ring of June was Sienna Miller's: a round brilliant solitaire, no halo, no pavé, on a bare band. The British press filed it under "old money", which mostly means it is not trying to impress.

It is the most useful reminder of the year. A ring does not need to be large to be right, and restraint is a choice rather than a budget constraint. OREA and its fine setting (€1,620) plays exactly that card, with a radiant rather than a round.

On the wedding side: the stack, with a deliberate gap

The wedding trend of the year comes not from a ring but from a stack. Through 2026 Zendaya was seen with, in turn, a plain gold band and then a fine eternity band worn alongside her engagement ring — and above all, a deliberate space between the two, instead of the millimetre-perfect nesting everyone was chasing until now.

That is excellent news in practice: the gap avoids gold rubbing against gold, and therefore wear, and it frees up the choice of band — you are no longer obliged to order a curved band that follows the setting. If you are hesitating between plain, pavé and full eternity, our guide compares all three; the all-the-way-round spirit appears on our tennis bracelet.

Another design that circulated widely this year: the Toi & Moi, two stones facing one another. Our MIA, two one-carat pears, starts at €2,260.

What the press figures are actually worth

A warning, because it saves disappointment. The carat weights reported in the media are estimates made from photographs, and they diverge wildly. Zoë Kravitz's ring has been estimated at anywhere between 5 and 12 carats depending on the publication. Sienna Miller's, between 1.5 and 3 carats — a factor of four on the price. Madison Beer's, between 10 and 15 carats.

Nobody can weigh a stone from a paparazzi shot. When you arrive at an appointment with a photograph saying "I would like that", the work starts there: identifying the shape, the ratio and the setting — which can be read — and dropping the assumed carat, which cannot.

The honest translation: the same decisions, at what price

This is the question everyone asks and nobody writes down. Take the year's decisions and look at what they cost in 18 carat gold with a certified diamond, here: a cushion brought forward by a halo starts at €2,650, an Asscher in a bezel at €1,900, a generous oval on a pavé setting at €2,600, a marquise at €2,360, a pear in an open bezel at €1,700.

The gap with the rings above is not a gap in craftsmanship: it is the carat of the centre stone, and who is paying for a shop window. The first lever is arbitrating the 4Cs, the second is natural or lab-grown, which moves 35 to 40 % on the stone. With both played well, the silhouette of a much discussed ring sits inside a €2,000 to €3,500 budget.

What to take from it

2026 rewards elongated stones, low settings and yellow gold, and finally leaves room for the small, the antique and the coloured. It is a fairly healthy year: less racing for carats, more attention to the drawing. Come with the photographs that stayed with you — the ones you saved say more about your taste than any list of trends, this one included.
