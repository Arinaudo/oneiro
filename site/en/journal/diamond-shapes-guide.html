Diamond shapes, and what each one changes on the finger

Published 8 July 2026 · 8 min read · by the Oneiro house

Shape is the one criterion of a diamond that cannot be measured. The 4Cs describe quality; shape decides character. It also changes, very concretely, the apparent size of the stone, its price at equal carat, and how it is worn day to day. Here is what to know before deciding.

The rule nobody states

At equal carat, the round brilliant is the most expensive shape — by 10 to 25 % over the others. Two reasons: it is the most requested, and its cut consumes the most rough material. So-called fancy shapes cost less and often look larger, because they are elongated and their visible surface per carat is greater.

In other words: choosing an oval over a round wins on both counts. It is not a compromise, it is a trade-off.

Choose for the hand, not for the fashion

Short fingers or slender hands: elongated shapes (oval, pear, marquise, emerald) stretch visually. Long fingers: everything works, and round or cushion shapes balance nicely. Active hands: avoid unprotected points, prefer a bezel or V-claws.

Three classic mistakes

  • Choosing a shape from a catalogue photograph. Two ovals of the same carat can have very different ratios — one will look elegant, the other stretched. The length-to-width ratio is a figure to ask for, just like the carat.
  • Neglecting clarity on step cuts. Emerald and Asscher do not have enough facets to hide an inclusion. On those two shapes only, go one grade up in clarity: it is the only case where we recommend it.
  • Forgetting the wedding band. A marquise or a pear set lengthways leaves little room for a straight band. Think about the stack from the engagement ring onwards: we cover it in the wedding band guide.

And if you are still hesitating

That is normal, and healthy: shape is the most personal decision in the project. We shortlist certified stones in two or three different shapes, at the same budget, and you compare photographs and certificates in hand. It is free, it is our job, and it settles the question in one session.
