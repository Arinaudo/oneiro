How to choose a diamond: the 4Cs guide, without the jargon

Published June 2026 · 6 min read · by the Oneiro house

Four criteria describe any diamond in the world — and determine its price. They fit into four words: Carat, Cut, Colour, Clarity. Once you understand them, you stop buying blind and start making intelligent trade-offs. Here is the guide we give our own clients.

Carat — the weight, not the perceived size

One carat = 0.20 grams. It is the best known criterion, and the most misused. Two things to know: the price does not climb proportionally but in steps (a 1.00 ct is worth considerably more than two 0.50 ct stones), and the visible diameter grows slowly (between 0.90 and 1.00 ct the eye sees almost nothing, the wallet very much does).

The trick that works: aim just under the symbolic thresholds — 0.90 rather than 1.00 ct, 1.40 rather than 1.50 ct. Identical effect on the finger, real saving.

Cut — the criterion that creates the sparkle

The cut measures the precision of the proportions, the symmetry and the polish. It is what turns light into brilliance. A large, badly cut diamond looks dull; a medium stone cut to excellence lights up a room. It is the one criterion we recommend never compromising on: aim for Excellent or Very Good, always.

Not to be confused with the shape (round brilliant, oval, pear, emerald, cushion and so on), which is a matter of taste — and which affects the price: at equal carat, the round brilliant is the most expensive, while fancy shapes often save 10 to 25 % and look larger.

Colour — whiteness, graded from D to Z

White diamonds run from D (perfectly colourless, the rarest) to Z (a visible yellowish tint). The truth that sellers of D-E-F stones do not enjoy: between D and H, the difference is invisible to an untrained eye, especially once the stone is set. A well cut G or H is, for us, the best beauty-to-price ratio. On yellow or rose gold you can even go down to I-J without losing anything — the metal warms the perception anyway.

Clarity — purity, and the art of paying for what can be seen

Almost every diamond contains tiny natural marks, called inclusions. The scale runs from IF (nothing visible under a ×10 loupe) to I (inclusions visible to the naked eye). Between the two lies the sensible zone: VS1-VS2, or VVS if the budget allows — inclusions invisible to the naked eye, at a reasonable price. Paying for IF means paying for a perfection nobody will ever see without a microscope.

How to arbitrate: our order of priority

  • 1. Cut first — Excellent, non-negotiable: that is the brilliance.
  • 2. Carat according to the effect you want — playing just under the thresholds.
  • 3. Colour G-H — invisible to the eye, very visible to the budget.
  • 4. Clarity VS — clean to the naked eye, without overpaying.

And always: insist on an independent GIA or IGI certificate — never an "in-house certificate". It is the certificate that attests the 4Cs and protects your purchase. At Oneiro, every centre stone comes with one, whether it is natural or lab-grown.
